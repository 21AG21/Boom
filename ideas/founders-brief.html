01

The one finding that changes the plan

Load-bearing · from the market research

A company called Moonbird already sells a $199 handheld breathing pacer.

It's a silicone device that expands and contracts in your hand to pace your breathing. This is the closest thing to Breathe Stone that exists — and it's the best news in this brief, for two reasons.

It de-risks demand: people already pay $199 for a handheld tactile breathing pacer, so the category is proven, not hypothetical. And it hands us our wedge: Moonbird is a $199 gadget in a case that deforms as a whole. Breathe Stone wins by being a $34 stone you keep in your pocket (a fifth the price), by moving only a small thumb pad on an otherwise ordinary object, and by reaching bulk/waiting-room channels a $199 device never can.

It also reshaped the patent: the draft now cites Moonbird as prior art and claims the narrower, defensible ground — the localized pad, the silent-hold method, and a battery-free version.
